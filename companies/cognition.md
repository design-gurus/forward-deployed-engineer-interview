# Cognition Forward Deployed Engineer Interview

The Cognition loop is commonly reported to have six stages, and four of them simulate customer work. It has no round built around a hard algorithm. Candidates run a real task in the company's own product, present a past project, pitch the product to a panel playing executives, and hold a simulated customer call. Sources for this loop are few, so confirm every stage with your recruiter.

## The stages

Devin is Cognition's AI software engineer, a product that writes, tests, and fixes code. Job postings also use the title "Deployed Engineer".

| Stage | Reported format | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiter screen | A call with a non-technical recruiter | Motivation, background, and clear communication |
| Technical take-home | A hands-on exercise in Devin, with little code | Product understanding and customer value |
| Project presentation | About 45 minutes, to a panel | Impact, design reasons, and composure |
| Leadership one-on-ones | Conversations with senior leaders | Speaking clearly with senior people, and judgment |
| Executive pitch | A panel that plays company executives | Explaining business value |
| Customer call | A timed preparation window, then a live simulated call | Fast structuring and real-time answers |

## The take-home and the presentation

The take-home tests whether you can run a real workflow in a developer tool and explain what a customer gets from it. A good write-up names the task, what the tool completed, what you had to correct, and how long each part took.

In the presentation, some panelists may play a less technical or impatient customer. State the project's impact within the first five minutes. See [the project deep dive](../rounds/project-deep-dive.md).

## The executive pitch

Frame the product around outcomes that executives measure: faster delivery, lower engineering cost, and time freed for harder work. A feature list without a business outcome is reported as the most common weak answer.

An outcome is convincing with arithmetic behind it. Suppose a customer has 40 engineers, and each spends about 4 hours a week on routine work such as dependency upgrades. That is 160 engineer-hours a week. Do not promise a share of those hours. Propose a four-week pilot on one team of 8 engineers, which covers 32 hours a week, and measure how many routine tasks the tool completes and how long review takes. A measured result persuades an executive more than a promised one.

## The simulated customer call

You receive a fictional company's problems, a timed preparation window, and then a live call. The window's length is not reported. A plan for 20 minutes:

| Minutes | Task | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 | Read the material and find the user and the decision | One sentence: who decides what |
| 5 to 10 | List the data the company has and the data it lacks | Two short lists |
| 10 to 15 | Choose the smallest first step that delivers value | One proposal with one measure |
| 15 to 20 | Write five questions for the customer | Five questions, most important first |

When the customer asks something unexpected, answer briefly and connect it to one of your questions. A customer who asks about cost in the first minute gets an honest short answer: "I need to know your volume before I can estimate it." Then ask the volume question.

## Other AI startups

Reports from other AI startups describe loops of four or five stages, and most include a customer case round. Two patterns repeat across them: a customer's problem solved out loud, and practical coding instead of puzzles. Prepare the customer rounds first.

## An 18-hour plan

| Week | Focus | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Use the product on a real task, and note what a customer would pay for | 6 |
| 2 | Two timed customer calls with a friend, and one executive pitch with arithmetic | 6 |
| 3 | A 45-minute project talk and three ownership and conflict stories | 6 |
